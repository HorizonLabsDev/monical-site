Plan your fixed costs in a calendar: never lose track of a payment again

By Horizon Labs · October 23, 2026 · 4 min read

Rent on the 1st, Netflix on the 12th, insurance at the end of the quarter: each payment is harmless on its own. Together they leave you with less at month's end than you expected. With a calendar as your base, you see the dates before the money leaves your account.

Why fixed costs and subscriptions are so easy to lose track of

Payments are spread across the month and repeat at different rhythms: monthly, quarterly, yearly. Many subscriptions renew automatically without you doing anything, which is exactly why they go unnoticed. A bank balance only shows what is there right now. It doesn't show what will still leave your account in the coming weeks.

Step 1: Collect all recurring payments

Take half an hour once and go through your bank statements from the last three to six months. Note everything that repeats:

  • Housing: rent, electricity, internet, mobile contract
  • Insurance and memberships, including ones that come only once a year
  • Subscriptions: streaming, music, cloud storage, gym, newspapers
  • Instalments and loans

Don't forget subscriptions billed through Apple, Google or PayPal. On a bank statement they often appear as a single lump item.

Step 2: Enter every payment with its due date

Enter each payment on the day it is charged and set the rhythm. This builds a monthly calendar where you can see at a glance which days are expensive. If charges pile up around the start of the month, you know your account has to be well filled there. This overview of how payments are distributed is exactly what a plain list lacks.

Step 3: Set a reminder before the payment

A reminder one to three days before the charge gives you time to check your account. For yearly fees and insurance, a longer lead time of a week or more is worthwhile. Then you can still cancel or compare offers before the contract renews.

Step 4: Set a monthly budget for variable spending

You can plan fixed costs. Groceries, restaurants or shopping fluctuate. A monthly budget per category helps there. Don't create one for everything, only for the two or three areas where you are most likely to overspend. Check how much of the budget is used up and whether that fits the point in the month. If half the budget is gone in the first week, you see it early enough to adjust.

Step 5: Review and clear out once a month

Set a fixed date, for example the last Sunday of the month, and go through the list:

  • Which subscriptions did I not use this month?
  • Which contracts expire soon?
  • Does the total of my fixed costs still fit my income?

Even one cancelled subscription you won't miss gives back a noticeable amount over a year.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find all my subscriptions?

Go through your bank statements from the last three to six months and mark everything that repeats. Also check your credit card, PayPal and the subscriptions in your Apple ID settings.

How many days before a payment should I be reminded?

For monthly payments, one to three days is often enough so your account is covered. For yearly fees and insurance, a week or more lets you still cancel or compare.

What is the difference between fixed costs and variable spending?

Fixed costs are the same amount on fixed dates, such as rent, insurance or streaming. Variable spending fluctuates, such as groceries, restaurants or clothing. For fixed costs you plan dates, for variable spending a monthly budget.
